## Official requirements (summary)
- **Advanced degree:** a U.S. advanced degree (or foreign equivalent), or a bachelor's degree plus at least 5 years of progressive experience in the specialty [[fonte:uscis-eb2]].
- **Exceptional ability:** expertise significantly above the ordinary in the sciences, arts, or business, meeting at least 3 of 6 criteria: degree in the field, employer letters showing 10+ years of experience, professional license, salary showing the ability, professional memberships, and recognition from peers or organizations [[fonte:uscis-eb2]].
- Usually requires a job offer and a **labor certification** approved by the Department of Labor (DOL); the employer files Form I-140. Exceptions exist (Schedule A occupations and the National Interest Waiver) [[fonte:uscis-eb2]] [[fonte:uscis-permanent-workers]].

## Family
Once the I-140 is approved, the spouse and unmarried children under 21 may apply for admission (E-21 and E-22 status) [[fonte:uscis-eb2]].

## What you can do now
- Find out how your degree is seen in the U.S. (credential evaluation).
- Gather employer letters showing your experience.
- Take your questions to a licensed **immigration attorney**: they evaluate your case and handle the process.
