## Official requirements (summary)
- **Skilled workers:** jobs requiring at least 2 years of training or experience [[fonte:uscis-eb3]].
- **Professionals:** a U.S. bachelor's degree or foreign equivalent, in a job that normally requires one; experience cannot replace the degree [[fonte:uscis-eb3]].
- **Other workers:** jobs requiring less than 2 years of training or experience, not temporary or seasonal [[fonte:uscis-eb3]].
- All require a permanent, full-time job offer. Most require a DOL-approved labor certification; the employer files Form I-140 [[fonte:uscis-eb3]] [[fonte:uscis-permanent-workers]].

## Family
Once the I-140 is approved, the spouse and unmarried children under 21 may apply for permanent residence, with the principal or later [[fonte:uscis-eb3]].

## What you can do now
- Update your résumé to the U.S. format and look for employers that sponsor.
- Take your questions to a licensed **immigration attorney**: they evaluate your case and handle the process.
