## Official requirements (summary)
- The occupation requires highly specialized knowledge and generally at least a bachelor's degree in the specialty [[fonte:uscis-h1b]].
- The worker generally needs a U.S. bachelor's degree or foreign equivalent in the field (or a state license, or a combination of education and experience) [[fonte:uscis-h1b]].
- The employer gets a Labor Condition Application (LCA) from DOL and files Form I-129 with USCIS [[fonte:uscis-h1b]].
- **Annual cap:** 65,000, plus 20,000 for people with a U.S. master's degree or higher. Cap-subject petitions need a selected electronic registration (registration typically opens in March). Higher-education institutions and certain research organizations are not subject to the cap [[fonte:uscis-h1b]].
- Initial stay of up to 3 years, extendable to 6 years in total [[fonte:uscis-h1b]].
- **Note:** the official page describes a $100,000 payment for certain petitions (Proclamation of September 19, 2025) and a court order of June 8, 2026 on the subject; check the current status at the source [[fonte:uscis-h1b]].

## Family
The spouse and unmarried children under 21 may seek H-4 status. Certain H-4 spouses may apply for work authorization once the H-1B worker has started the employment-based green card process [[fonte:uscis-h1b]].

## What you can do now
- Look for employers in your field that hire with H-1B.
- Take your questions to a licensed **immigration attorney**: they evaluate your case and handle the process.
