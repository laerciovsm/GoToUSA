GoToUSA is **support for your journey**. Guardianship is a **court process**, conducted by an **attorney**. Whether it's needed, and with what limits, is assessed by the attorney and, in the end, decided by the judge. This applies to adult children with disabilities and also to elderly parents or grandparents who can no longer decide on their own. Here are Brazil's official rules so you arrive prepared.

## When guardianship applies

- People who, for a temporary or permanent reason, **cannot express their will** are subject to guardianship [[fonte:br-civil-code]].
- Guardianship is an extraordinary protective measure and affects only property and business acts [[fonte:br-lbi]].
- The alternative, **supported decision-making** (tomada de decisão apoiada), is requested **by the person themselves**, who chooses at least 2 supporters [[fonte:br-civil-code]]. That's why, when the person can't express their will, the attorney assesses guardianship.

## How the request works

- **Who can request it:** the spouse or partner, relatives or tutors, the representative of the facility where the person lives, or the Public Prosecutor's Office [[fonte:br-cpc]].
- **What the request includes:** the facts that show the incapacity and since when it appeared, and a **medical report** (or an explanation of why it couldn't be attached). In urgent cases, the judge may appoint a provisional guardian for specific acts [[fonte:br-cpc]].
- **Interview with the judge:** the judge talks with the person about their life, wishes, preferences, and family ties. The interview can take place **where the person is**, with a specialist present and with **tools that help communication**. Relatives and people close to them may be heard [[fonte:br-cpc]].
- **Expert assessment:** it can be done by a team of several professionals and states for which acts guardianship is needed [[fonte:br-cpc]].
- **Ruling:** the judge appoints the guardian and sets the **limits** of the guardianship, considering the person's wishes and preferences. The ruling is recorded at the civil registry and published [[fonte:br-cpc]].
- **End of guardianship:** if the cause no longer exists, guardianship can be ended, fully or partly [[fonte:br-cpc]].

## Who can be the guardian

- The spouse or partner (not separated) is the guardian; without one, the father or mother; without them, the most suitable descendant. If there is none, the judge chooses [[fonte:br-civil-code]].
- For a person with a disability, the judge may set up **shared guardianship** among more than one person [[fonte:br-civil-code]].
- The guardian reports to the judge every year [[fonte:br-lbi]].

## For the move to the U.S.

- For the Brazilian passport of a legally incapable adult, the Federal Police asks for the guardian's consent (or a court authorization) [[fonte:pf-in-173]]. The details are in the task **Legal representation for the passport and the visa**.
- In USCIS requests, the legal guardian must have been appointed by a court or public authority and prove it with documentation, such as the court order [[fonte:uscis-signatures]]. Ask the **immigration attorney** how to present the Brazilian ruling.
- If the person isn't Brazilian, their country's rules apply: talk to an attorney there.
- Note the progress in the **Record**. Don't write down case numbers, document numbers, or passwords.
